# Purchase Timing Analysis — Key Findings

## The Core Question

Braves revenue leadership asked whether fans are increasingly buying single-game tickets closer to game day compared to prior seasons. If the purchase window is shrinking, it would have implications for pricing strategy, inventory management, and promotional timing.

## Answer: No Meaningful Shift in Timing

Despite significant changes in total volume, the **proportional timing mix has remained remarkably stable** across all four seasons (2023–2026). When you look at what percentage of average daily seats sold falls in each time window - day of, 1–2 days before, 3–7 days, 8–21 days, and 21+ days - the bars are nearly identical heights across years. The extra volume in 2026 is spread proportionally across all windows, not concentrated in any one bucket.

## Volume Is Up

2026 is the highest year for single-game seats sold in every single day bucket. This represents a broad-based increase in demand, not a shift in when people buy. Even the lower-volume windows (21+ days out) are up, meaning fans are buying more tickets at every lead time.

## Conversion Rates Are Improving

The ratio of single-game transactions to primary site hits has improved steadily, with 2026 showing the highest conversion rate across all day buckets. More site visitors are completing purchases. This pattern holds for both higher-tier games (Diamond/Marquee/Premier) and lower-tier games (Select/Standard/Value).

## Site Traffic Patterns

Primary site traffic (Ticketmaster event detail pages) dipped in 2025 but rebounded in 2026 to near 2023/2024 levels. Secondary market traffic (SeatGeek) has declined year over year. Combined with rising primary conversion rates, this may suggest fans are shifting toward the primary market for purchases.

## Tier-Level Consistency

Both higher-tier and lower-tier game groups exhibit the same stable timing distribution. The pattern isn't driven by one category of games - it's a consistent fan behavior across the full schedule.

## What This Means

The team does not need to adjust strategy for a "last-minute buying" trend - there isn't one. Promotional timing and pricing windows can continue to assume a consistent lead-time distribution. The improving conversion rate is a positive signal that the purchase experience and/or pricing is effective at turning browsing into buying.
